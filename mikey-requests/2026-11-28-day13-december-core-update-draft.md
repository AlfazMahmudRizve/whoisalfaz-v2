## Draft

Google has never warned anyone about a core update before it started. On 24 November, it did.

The November 2026 core update finished that morning. It began on 10 November at 10:06 US/Pacific and ended on 24 November at 6:03 US/Pacific — close to 14 days. The Search Status Dashboard entry reads: "The rollout was complete as of November 24, 2026."

About an hour later, Google opened a new entry on the same dashboard. It was not a rollout. It was a notice: the December 2026 core update, preannounced. Google has always announced core updates on the day they start. This is the first time it has given advance warning of one.

The notice is short. Google says the December update focuses on "multiple improvements to how our systems understand and rank relevant content (including for longer and more complex questions), as well as improving personalized results for people who have personalization turned on." It applies to all locations and languages where Google Search is available, and Google's usual guidance on core updates applies. On timing, the dashboard says only that the update "is scheduled to be released in December 2026." No day, no length.

It is not live. A Google spokesperson told Search Engine Roundtable there is no date yet, that Google will announce it "the day it starts rolling out", and that "it's not imminent." So do not expect it next week — and do not blame any ranking change this week on December. Nothing has rolled out yet.

Why the warning now? On the same day, Google updated a Search Central page about how it keeps site owners informed. The page says Google gives notice "when an update introduces actionable policy requirements or may cause broad ranking shifts for site owners". It names one specific reason: for changes where UK users may see a significant ranking shift, it gives advance notice "in accordance with CMA's fair ranking conduct requirements for Google". The CMA is the UK's Competition and Markets Authority. The page does not say December was preannounced because of the CMA specifically — but the rule is there, and this update is the first one it has been applied to.

That leaves a gap between the two updates, and the gap is the useful part. November is done. December is coming. What you do in between decides how December treats you.

First, do not judge November yet. Google's own guidance says to wait at least a full week after a core update completes before analysing your site in Search Console. November completed on 24 November, so that week ends around 1 December. Any reading taken before then is noise — the rollout only just stopped moving.

Second, when the week is up, measure November properly. Compare the week before 10 November with the week after 24 November, query by query. The question is not "did traffic fall" — it is "which queries fell, and which pages fell with them". A core update does not target a site or a page; it re-ranks content. The damage, when there is any, shows up as specific queries your pages used to win and no longer do. That is what the [November update piece](https://whoisalfaz.me/blog/google-november-2026-core-update-what-changed/) covered when it finished — and now the numbers behind it are ready to be read.

Third, find the gaps. For every query you lost, someone else gained it. That is what a keyword gap is: the queries your competitors rank for that you do not. Before December lands, the job is to list those gaps, pick the ones that matter, and close them — either by strengthening the page that used to rank, or by writing the page that should have ranked all along. A gap tool makes this concrete: put your domain next to a competitor's and it shows the keywords they hold that you are missing. The [keyword gap analyzer](https://fasttools.me/keyword-gap-analyzer) does exactly that, and the list it returns is the work plan for the gap between updates.

Fourth, put the check on a schedule. The comparison that matters — this week against the week before the update — is a job that repeats. A scheduled workflow that pulls your Search Console numbers every week and keeps the comparison current means you are not scrambling to rebuild it when Google announces the December start date. The announcement will come on the day the rollout begins; by then, the measuring should already be done. That is the kind of repeating check [n8n](https://whoisalfaz.me/go/n8n) is built for.

November was the third core update of 2026. The March–April and May–June updates each took around 12 days; November took close to 14. December has no date, no stated length, and no impact to attribute yet — anyone claiming December has already moved their rankings is guessing.

The point is not the date. It is that Google gave you something it has never given before: time. A core update used to arrive with no warning and a two-week rollout. This one arrives with a warning and no date. Use the warning. Measure November, find the gaps, and close them before December starts.
