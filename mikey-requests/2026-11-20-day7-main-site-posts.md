### Body (Claude Meetings)

Claude now sits in your Google Meet call as a visible guest, takes the notes, and starts the document before the call ends. The catch is who gets it: Team and Enterprise plans only, Meet only, and it is still a beta.

I have not sat in a call with Claude yet — I do not have a Team plan, and neither do most readers of this post. What follows is what Anthropic's announcement and Help Center say, checked on 17 November 2026, with the gaps marked where Anthropic has not answered yet.

## What it is

Anthropic announced Claude Meetings on 12 November 2026. It is a beta feature that adds Claude to a Google Meet call as a named participant. It follows the discussion, writes a transcript, notes and action items, answers questions asked out loud or in the call chat, and can build a document, whiteboard or slides while the meeting is still running. After the call, everything sits on a meeting page in claude.ai.

Two points in that description matter. First, Claude is visible — it joins as a participant, posts a notice in the call chat when it arrives, and everyone on the call can see it is there. Second, it works during the call, not after it. The older pattern was a bot that sent a summary ten minutes after everyone left. Claude Meetings starts the work product while people are still talking.

Anthropic first described it as "coming soon" on 10 November, in the same update that introduced Claude Sheets and Whiteboards. The beta itself started rolling out two days later, on 12 November.

## Who can use it

Only people on a Team or Enterprise plan, and only on Google Meet. Personal plans — Free, Pro, Max and Ultra — do not get it. Anthropic says other video platforms are coming soon but has not named them or given a date, so Zoom and Teams users wait.

It is also not offered at all to some organisations: those using zero data retention, a HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys cannot use it during the beta. If your team runs one of those setups, the feature simply is not there.

There is no separate price for Claude Meetings during the beta. Notes, transcripts and answers are included in the Team and Enterprise plans. Documents and whiteboards made in a call are free through 10 February 2027. Anthropic has not said what they will cost after that date, or what the meeting records will cost once the beta ends.

Anthropic says Claude can stay in a call for up to eight hours. That is longer than any meeting should run, but it means a full-day workshop is inside the limit.

## The settings that decide how it behaves

The defaults differ by plan, and they are the first thing to check.

On Team plans the feature is on by default, and an owner can turn it off. On Enterprise plans an owner must enable it — it does not switch itself on.

Meetings with outside guests are the sharp edge. By default, Claude stays out of calls that include outside guests, and on Enterprise the default is internal participants only. That is a sane default for client calls, but it means the feature will quietly not show up in exactly the meetings where an agency most wants notes — unless an admin changes the setting on purpose.

One more detail from the Help Center: Claude saves a transcript, a summary and screen descriptions — not audio. Anthropic has not said how long meeting records are kept. That is a real gap for anyone who has to answer a client or a compliance question about where call records live.

## What I would check before turning it on

If you run a Team or Enterprise plan on Google Meet, four checks before the first real call:

1. **Confirm the default on your plan.** Team: on unless an owner turned it off. Enterprise: off unless an owner turned it on. Do not assume.
2. **Decide the guest rule in writing.** If Claude joins client calls, tell the client first — it announces itself in the chat, so a surprise is worse than a short heads-up.
3. **Test it on an internal call first.** Watch what the notes look like, what the action items miss, and whether the live document it builds is worth keeping or needs a rewrite.
4. **Ask where the records go.** Anthropic has not published a retention period. Until it does, treat the meeting page as a working note, not an archive.

## Who it is for

It is for teams already paying for Claude Team or Enterprise who spend their day in Google Meet: product teams, ops teams, agencies running internal stand-ups and planning calls. For them it removes the worst part of meetings — the fifteen minutes after, reconstructing what was agreed.

It is not for anyone on a personal Claude plan, anyone on Zoom or Teams, or any team on a zero-retention or HIPAA-ready setup. For those readers the honest answer is: wait. The beta is just over a week old, the platform list is one item long, and the price after February 2027 is still blank.

The idea itself is sound — an assistant that works during the meeting instead of summarising it after. Whether it is worth the plan it rides on depends on how many of your meetings happen in Meet, and how much you trust a transcript you did not write. Test it on internal calls, keep the guest rule tight, and check back when Anthropic names the next platform and the price after the beta.

In beta on 12 November 2026, for Claude Team and Enterprise plans, on Google Meet only.

No. Personal plans — Free, Pro, Max and Ultra — do not get it. It is Team and Enterprise only.

### Body (core update)

Google started its November core update on 10 November. The honest answer to "what changed" is: Google has not said. Here is what it did confirm, what to do, and what not to do while rankings move.

## What Google confirmed

The whole public record fits in a short list. Google's Search Status Dashboard posted the update on 10 November 2026 at 10:06 a.m. Pacific time, and five facts come with it:

- **Start:** 10 November 2026, 10:06 a.m. PT.
- **Scope:** global, all languages.
- **Duration:** the rollout may take up to two weeks, which puts the latest finish around 24 November.
- **Type:** a broad core update — a change to how Google ranks search results, not a penalty aimed at one kind of site.
- **Description:** Google called it "a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites."

That is the third core update of 2026. The March update ran 27 March to 8 April, and the May update ran 21 May to 2 June. This is the first broad core update in about five and a half months, and it is still rolling out — the dashboard entry has no end date yet.

## What Google did not say

Everything else in circulation right now is commentary, not confirmation. Google has not said what this update changes, which ranking systems it touches, which kinds of sites or queries it affects, whether it touches Discover or AI features, or whether it will finish inside the two-week window. Two of Google's last five core updates ran longer than the estimate, so 24 November is a working date, not a promise.

That matters because the usual flood of "winners and losers" lists is already out, a week into a rollout that is not finished. A page that dropped on 12 November may be back a week later without anyone touching it. Judging a page mid-rollout is reading a race at the halfway mark.

## What to do now

Google's own guidance for core updates is short, and it is the best plan available:

1. **Annotate 10 November in Search Console.** One minute, and every chart after that shows where the rollout started. Compare a full week after the rollout ends with the week before it started — 3 to 9 November.
2. **Save a baseline now.** Export the queries and pages that matter today, before more of the rollout lands. Without a baseline you cannot tell a real move from noise.
3. **Do not rewrite pages mid-rollout.** If a page dropped, wait. Fixing a page that was never broken is how a small dip becomes a real one.
4. **Wait a full week after Google marks the rollout complete.** Google's guide says to wait at least a full week after a core update completes, then compare that week with the week before the update started. If the rollout ends around 24 November, the first clean comparison week runs into early December — around 1 December.

For a small site the practical version is simpler: check once a day, write down what moved, change nothing until the dashboard says the rollout is done.

## Two things that will muddy your charts

Two separate events sit inside the same window, and both will show up in your data as if the core update caused them.

First, Google's Search Console Generative AI report undercounted impressions from 9 to 11 November because of a logging error. If your AI-mode numbers look low for those three days, that is the bug, not the update.

Second, Gemini 4 Hydrogen became the default model in AI Mode on 12 November. That is a model change in AI Mode, not part of the core update, but it landed two days after the rollout started. Do not read AI Mode traffic moves from that week as core-update moves.

## Why the timing is awkward

If the rollout uses its full two weeks, it ends around 24 November — three days before Black Friday on 27 November, with Thanksgiving on the 26th. Shops and publishers head into their biggest sales week with rankings still settling, and the first clean post-update comparison will not exist until about 1 December. That is bad timing, and it is also the reason not to panic-rewrite product pages this week: the rankings you see on Black Friday may not be the rankings you keep in December.
